# Antenatal and postnatal source comparison

Research checked 10 September 2026. This is a review note, not a personalised
appointment schedule. ANC means care during pregnancy; PNC means care after birth.
The WHO pages below are linked for comparison, not copied into the retrieval corpus.

| Source and context | What it describes | Required interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| NHM CHO booklet, February 2022, page 11 | First-trimester registration and four antenatal checks in the VHSND programme. | A programme statement is not a maximum number of clinically appropriate contacts. |
| PIB PMSMA publication, 8 June 2026, pages 2-4 | Care on the ninth day of each month at designated government facilities during the second/third trimester; extended follow-up for higher-risk pregnancies. | Supplementary programme information, not a replacement for routine care or a guarantee of local availability. |
| WHO ANC recommendations, 2016, current WHO summary | Eight contacts: first by 12 weeks, followed by 20, 26, 30, 34, 36, 38 and 40 weeks. | Do not silently replace an Indian programme description with a global schedule; obtain current local clinical reconciliation. |
| NHM CHO booklet, page 11 | Facility births: day 3, 7, 14, 21, 28 and 42 home visits; home births include an additional day-1 visit. | Preserve the delivery-setting condition. These home visits are distinct from immediate facility care. |
| NHM My Safe Motherhood, page 16 | Mother/baby health-worker check on birth day, day 3, day 7 and six weeks. | Older booklet with different contact framing; currency and local application need review. |
| WHO postnatal guidance, 2022 | Care in the first 24 hours and additional healthy-mother/newborn contacts at 48-72 hours, days 7-14 and week 6; more care when risk requires. | Do not merge different contact types into an invented universal schedule or advise waiting with symptoms. |
